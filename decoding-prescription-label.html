Decoding a Prescription Label

At some point in your life, you've probably seen one of those orange prescription bottles — laying on the street, sitting on a counter at home, or on a shelf inside a pharmacy. Wherever you've seen it, most (if not all) share one thing: a prescription label. A prescription label packs in several details about you, the pharmacy that filled it, and the medication itself. Below is a breakdown of each field.

Name of medication
Helps patients know what drug they're taking.
Patient's name & date of birth
Identification purposes.
Home address of patient
Can help prescribers and pharmacists know where to mail drugs, if necessary.
Patient's phone number
For calling in emergencies.
Directions
Helps the patient know how much to take, and how often.
RX number
An identification number that helps the pharmacy track refills, if necessary.
Quantity
How many tablets or capsules are in the bottle.
Refill frequency
Lets the patient know whether they need a refill of the medication.
Prescriber
The clinician who prescribed the medication.
Fill & expiration date
Dates showing when the bottle was filled and when the medication expires.
Pharmacy contact information
Helps patients reach the pharmacy with questions or updates on refills and orders.
