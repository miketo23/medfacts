Finishing Your Prescription

Have you ever dealt with a bacterial illness and started feeling better after taking the right antibiotics for a few days? While I haven't personally dealt with a bacterial illness, "feeling better" sounds great. But when you look at the medication you have left, it's often more than you expected. What's the next step? Whatever you decide, don't stop taking your antibiotics.

Some may wonder why that's the case. Even if you don't feel or see symptoms anymore, that doesn't mean the infection is completely gone. If you stop too soon, you let some of the surviving bacteria continue to multiply — and the bacteria that survive tend to be stronger, and more resistant to that antibiotic, than the ones that didn't.

That resistance is exactly what lets an infection come back harder to treat. Bacteria can, roughly, double in number every 20 minutes. Hypothetically, across 24 hours that could mean 2⁷², or about 4.72 × 10²¹ times the original size — a number that doesn't account for your immune system doing its job, but still makes the point: bacteria can grow fast. If an infection comes back after resistance has developed, there are fewer antibiotic options left to treat it.

So finish your prescription, unless your doctor tells you otherwise — and don't be afraid to ask your doctor or pharmacist about anything that's unclear. It's better to know what you're taking and how it can help or harm you. Finishing the course is also one of the simplest ways to protect yourself and others from antibiotic resistance.
